How to delete blurry photos in bulk on Android

The shaky photo of the dog, the third out-of-focus shot of a dinner, the picture your phone took by itself in your pocket… Blurry photos pile up without you noticing, and reviewing thousands by hand isn't realistic. This guide shows how to find and delete them in bulk without losing the good ones.

Updated October 31, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer
  1. By hand: open your gallery, sort by date and review bursts and repeated shots, where most blurry photos hide. It works, but it's slow.
  2. In bulk: use an app that measures each photo's sharpness and groups the blurry ones, like DupeFire's Smart Cleanup. Review the selection with preview before deleting.
  3. Watch for false alarms: a night shot, a portrait with a blurred background or an artistic photo can look blurry without being a mistake. Always check the preview.

Why blurry photos pile up

You don't delete them because you never look at them again. They mostly come from:

  • Bursts and repeated shots: you take five photos, only one comes out right, and the other four stay.
  • Low light: cameras lengthen the exposure and any movement blurs the shot.
  • Accidental photos: of the floor, the ceiling or your pocket.
  • Photos of screens or documents taken out of focus that you then retake properly.

A blurry photo takes up as much space as a good one (sometimes a bit less) and you'll never look at it again, so it's one of the safest things to delete. The hard part is finding them.

Method 1: find them by hand

  1. Open your gallery in grid view and sort by date: repeated shots end up next to each other.
  2. Enter selection mode and tick the ones that look shaky or out of focus. Strong blur is easy to spot in a small grid.
  3. Check the camera and screenshots folders in particular.
  4. Delete, then empty the trash to get the space back.

It's the most reliable method because you decide, but with thousands of photos it takes forever. Some galleries or manufacturer cleaner apps suggest low-quality photos; if yours does, use it, though options vary by model.

Combine it with a duplicate clean-up

Many blurry photos have a good twin right next to them. If you first remove duplicate and near-identical photos, there's less left to review. Then move on to old screenshots, which are often another big pile of nothing, and check whether you need to compress the photos you want to keep.

How to stop them piling up again

  • Check right after the shot. If it came out shaky, delete it on the spot.
  • Turn off burst saving if you don't use it, or pick the best shot and discard the rest.
  • Clean in cycles. A pass every month is far less work than a yearly clean-up.

FAQ

How does an app know a photo is blurry?

It measures the sharpness of the image's edges: if there is hardly any contrast at the edges, the photo is out of focus or shaky. It doesn't understand what's in the photo, so it can flag deliberately blurred shots; that's why you should check the preview.

Can it delete good photos by mistake?

It can flag some that aren't bad, which is why nothing is deleted without your confirmation. Review the selection before accepting and untick anything you want to keep.

Are they also deleted from Google Photos?

If backup is on, what you delete on the phone is also reflected in your Google Photos library when it syncs. Check the Google Photos trash if you want to recover something.
