How to find duplicate photos on Android

Camera bursts, the same photo in WhatsApp and in your gallery, repeated screenshots, backups… over time a phone collects thousands of repeated photos that take up gigabytes. This guide shows where they are and how to find them, with and without an app.

Why your phone has duplicate photos

Duplicates rarely mean you did something wrong. They pile up on their own in several ways:

  • Messaging apps. WhatsApp and Telegram save a copy of every image you receive and another of the ones you send. If the same meme reaches you in three groups, you have three files.
  • Bursts and live photos. The camera saves several near-identical shots and sometimes edited versions next to the original.
  • Repeated screenshots of the same conversation, map or ticket.
  • Manual copies: photos you moved from your computer that were already on the phone, or that you downloaded twice from email.
  • Backup and editing apps that keep their own version in another folder.

There are two kinds and it helps to tell them apart: exact duplicates (the same file byte for byte, even if the name changes) and near-duplicates (they look the same, but the file differs because it was recompressed, cropped or edited). The second kind is far more common, and no search by name or size will find it.

Where to look first

Open the Files app (or your phone's file manager) and check these folders, which collect the most repetition:

  • DCIM/Camera: camera photos and videos, including bursts.
  • Pictures and Pictures/Screenshots: screenshots and photos from other apps.
  • Download: images downloaded from the browser or email.
  • Android/media/com.whatsapp/WhatsApp/Media (and Telegram's): everything that arrives by messaging.

Sort each folder by name or size: two files with exactly the same size are usually the same photo. It's slow, but it works for exact copies if you don't have many photos.

Method 1: without installing anything

  1. Open Files by Google (it comes on many Android phones; otherwise it's on Google Play). Go to the Clean tab.
  2. If a duplicate files card appears, open it, review the list and tick the extra copies. Keep one of each.
  3. On Samsung phones, try Settings → Battery and device care → Storage, which sometimes suggests duplicate files. Other manufacturers have similar tools in their cleaner app.
  4. Empty the trash in your gallery or Google Photos: until then, the space isn't recovered.

These tools usually find only identical copies. They won't detect a photo recompressed by WhatsApp or two nearly identical shots, and the options vary by model and Android version.

After deleting: check this

  • Trash. Many galleries keep deleted items for 30 days. Empty it if you need the space now.
  • Backup. If you use Google Photos, changes also apply in the cloud. Check your Google storage in the app's settings.

FAQ

How do I know which duplicate photo to keep?

Usually the one with the highest resolution and the most recent date, or the one in the camera folder. DupeFire suggests one per group on those criteria, but you can always change it before deleting.

Can deleting duplicates remove the only copy of a photo?

Not if you make sure one in each group stays. In DupeFire every group always keeps at least one copy marked, and you confirm the deletion. Still, if a photo is important, back it up before cleaning.

Is Files by Google enough?

It's a good free first pass for exact copies. If you also want to find recompressed, cropped or near-identical photos, you need a tool that compares the image content.
