How to compress photos without losing quality on Android

A current phone photo can weigh 3 to 10 MB, and a minute of 4K video, several hundred. Compressing lets you keep your memories while taking up far less space. But there's a catch to "without losing quality". Here's what's possible and how to do it right.

Updated October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer
  1. Compressing a photo always discards some information, but at a medium-high level the difference is barely visible on screen and the file weighs much less.
  2. Options: lower the camera resolution, use Google Photos' storage saver (it affects the cloud copy) or compress on the phone with an app like DupeFire.
  3. Important: DupeFire saves a lighter copy and keeps the original. To really gain space you'll need to delete the originals once you've checked the copy is good enough.

Can you compress without losing quality?

Strictly speaking, not with phone JPEG photos: compressing discards details the eye barely notices. What you can do is choose a level where the loss isn't visible on a phone, a TV or a small print. If you'll enlarge a photo a lot or want to edit it, keep the original.

It's a good idea to compress what you won't edit anymore: photos of documents, receipts, whiteboards, forwarded videos, memories you want to keep but not view full screen.

Ways to reduce size without an app

  • Camera settings. On many phones you can pick a lower resolution or a more efficient format (HEIF/HEIC) in the camera settings. It only affects new photos.
  • Google Photos. In backup settings you can choose "Storage saver" quality, which compresses what you upload to the cloud. It doesn't shrink what's already on your phone until you use "Free up space".
  • Sharing. When you send through WhatsApp, the app already recompresses photos unless you send them as a document.

None of these shrinks the photos already saved on your phone. For that you need to compress them.

How to compress photos with DupeFire

  1. Install DupeFire from Google Play and grant access to your photos.
  2. On the home screen tap Compress photos and select the images you want to shrink.
  3. Choose the quality: High (~85%), Medium (~60%) or Low (~35%). For everyday use, High is hardly noticeable; Medium saves much more.
  4. Start the compression. DupeFire saves a lighter copy in Photos › DupeFire and doesn't touch the original.
  5. Compare the copy with the original. If it's good enough, delete the original to get the space back.

Until you delete the original you haven't gained space: you'll have both versions. Compression is a decision you make, photo by photo or in bulk.

And with videos

Videos give back the most space. In Compress videos you choose High (up to 1080p), Medium (up to 720p) or Low (up to 480p). The app saves a lighter H.264 copy in Videos › DupeFire and keeps the original. It takes longer than with photos, so let it work while the phone charges.

Before compressing: clean out what you don't need

Compressing a photo you'd delete anyway is a waste of time. Clean up first: delete duplicates, blurry photos and screenshots you don't need. Then compress what's left that you want to keep. More ideas in how to free up space without deleting apps.

FAQ

Does compressing photos reduce their quality?

Yes, some information is always discarded. At a high level the difference is very hard to see on screen; at a low level it's more noticeable, especially when zooming in.

Which compression level should I choose?

For memories and everyday photos, High. For documents, receipts or screenshots you only need to read, Medium. Low only if you need to save as much as possible.

Is the photo's date or location lost?

It depends on the app and the format. If you need to keep that data, check the compressed copy before deleting the original.
