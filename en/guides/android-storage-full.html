Android storage full? How to free up space

“Storage space running out.” The camera won't save, WhatsApp won't download and the system update won't install. Before you start deleting photos blindly, find out what's really using the space: there are almost always gigabytes to recover without touching anything you care about.

Updated October 21, 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer

Go to Settings → Storage to see which category is biggest. Then, in this order: delete duplicate photos and videos, clean up what WhatsApp and Telegram have saved, review your largest videos and files, remove old screenshots, clear the cache of the heaviest apps and uninstall the ones you don't use. You don't need a “booster” or a RAM cleaner.

1. Check what's using the space

Open Settings → Storage. On Samsung it's under Settings → Battery and device care → Storage (or Device care on older versions). You'll see used space split into categories: images, videos, audio, documents, apps, system and “other” or “misc files”.

  • Images and videos are usually the largest and the easiest to recover: that's steps 2 to 5.
  • Apps: step 6.
  • System is Android itself and can't be reduced. It's normal for it to take 10–20 GB or more.
  • Other is usually downloads, messaging app files and cache.
📏

Try to keep at least 10–15% of your storage free. With storage nearly full, Android slows down, apps crash and updates fail.

2. Duplicate photos and videos

This is the most “free” space you'll find: you lose no memories because the other copy stays. Duplicates build up on their own over time:

  • The same photo received in several WhatsApp groups.
  • Bursts and near-identical shots taken one after another.
  • Edited or cropped photos sitting next to the original.
  • Copies left behind when moving photos from another phone or a computer.

Finding them by hand in a gallery of thousands of photos is impossible. 3. WhatsApp and Telegram

Messaging apps are the number one source of clutter: memes, forwarded videos, stickers, voice notes… and a copy of every photo you send. WhatsApp has its own manager:

  1. In WhatsApp, go to Settings → Storage and data → Manage storage.
  2. Review “Forwarded many times” (memes and chain messages) and “Larger than 5 MB” (mostly videos).
  3. Below that, chats are sorted by how much space they use. Open the heaviest groups and delete in bulk.

What this manager can't see are repeated copies across chats or near-identical photos. We cover that in detail in how to delete duplicate WhatsApp photos. DupeFire also groups everything WhatsApp and Telegram have downloaded so you can review it in one place.

4. Large videos and files

A single five-minute 4K video can take more space than a thousand photos. Find your largest files and decide one by one:

  • The Files by Google app (or “My Files” on Samsung) has a large-files section.
  • DupeFire lists them sorted by size in Smart Cleanup.
  • If you want to keep a video but not at that size, DupeFire can re-encode it to 1080p, 720p or 480p and compress JPEG photos at three quality levels. The original isn't modified: you decide whether to delete it afterwards.
  • Check your Downloads folder too: APKs, PDFs and videos you grabbed once and forgot.

5. Screenshots and blurry photos

Screenshots pile up without you noticing: a boarding pass, a conversation, a recipe… and years later they're still there. The same goes for shaky or out-of-focus photos nobody will ever look at again.

In your gallery you can open the Screenshots album and select many at once. DupeFire finds them automatically and also detects blurry photos by analysing their sharpness, so you can review and delete them in one go.

6. Cache and apps you don't use

Clear the cache

Go to Settings → Apps, open the apps that use the most space (browser, social media, Spotify, maps…) and under Storage tap Clear cache. Don't tap “Clear data”: that wipes your login, settings and, in some apps, downloaded content.

The cache grows back as you use the app, so this is temporary relief, not a fix.

Uninstall or archive apps

In Play Store → your profile picture → Manage apps & device → Manage you can sort apps by size. Uninstall the ones you don't use. If your phone supports it, archiving an app frees almost all of its space but keeps your data for when you reinstall it.

7. If you use Google Photos

If backup is on, Google Photos can remove photos from your phone that are already safely in the cloud: in the app, tap your profile picture and look for “Free up space”. First check that the backup is complete and that you have room in your Google account.

Clean up duplicates first: otherwise you're also uploading, and paying storage for, the copies.

What not to do

  • “Booster” or “RAM cleaner” apps. Closing background apps doesn't free storage, and Android manages memory by itself. Many of those apps exist to show ads.
  • Deleting system folders with a file manager without knowing what they are.
  • Factory resetting as a first step: it works, but you lose everything if you don't have a backup.

FAQ

Why is storage still full after deleting photos?

Many galleries move deleted photos to a trash bin for 30 days. Empty it from the gallery or from Google Photos to actually get the space back.

What is “Other” or “Misc files”?

Everything Android doesn't classify as photo, video, audio or app: downloads, WhatsApp and Telegram files, caches and app data. Cleaning up messaging apps and the Downloads folder usually shrinks it a lot.
