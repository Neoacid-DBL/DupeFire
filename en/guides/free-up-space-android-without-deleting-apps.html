How to free up space on Android without deleting apps

What fills your phone is almost never the apps, but what's inside them: photos, videos, downloads and messaging files. This guide goes through where to recover space, from what gives back the most to the least, without uninstalling a single app.

Updated October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Short answer
  1. Open Settings → Storage and see which category weighs the most: it's almost always Images and videos or Files, not apps.
  2. Empty the gallery trash, delete duplicates and heavy videos, and clear screenshots and the Downloads folder.
  3. Last, clear the cache of the biggest apps. It's a temporary relief, so leave it for the end.

1. Check what's using the space

Go to Settings → Storage (or Device care on Samsung). You'll see the breakdown by category. If Apps takes less than Images, Videos or Files, uninstalling apps won't fix anything: the space is in your own files. For per-folder detail, Files by Google can sort by size.

2. Empty the trash

When you delete a photo, many galleries and Google Photos send it to a trash where it keeps using space for days (30 in most cases). If you cleaned up and storage didn't go down, empty it from the gallery itself. There's also a trash in Files by Google and, on Android 11 or later, at system level.

3. Delete duplicate photos and videos

It's the biggest saving with the least risk: copies add nothing. For exact ones, Files by Google's cleaner works; for near-identical ones (recompressed by WhatsApp, bursts, crops) use an app that compares content, like DupeFire: tap Scan for duplicates, review each group and confirm. The detailed steps are in how to find duplicate photos on Android.

4. Find the heaviest files and videos

A single 4K video can take more than a thousand photos. Sort Videos by size in the gallery or in Files and review the ones you no longer need: screen recordings, forwarded WhatsApp videos, recorded classes. In DupeFire, the Large tool on the home screen lists your heaviest files so you can review them at a glance.

If you want to keep a video but it weighs too much, compress it without a noticeable loss of quality instead of deleting it.

5. Screenshots and blurry photos

They're thousands of small files you never open again: used tickets, conversations, maps. Open Pictures/Screenshots and delete in batches, or use Screenshots and Blurry in DupeFire to see them grouped with a preview. More in how to delete blurry photos in bulk.

6. Downloads and messaging files

The Downloads folder piles up PDFs, installers and files you opened once. Review it in Files. And messaging apps keep everything they receive: see what to delete when WhatsApp fills your phone.

Last: the cache (and why it's not the fix)

In Settings → Apps, open the biggest apps and tap Clear cache (not "Clear data": that signs you out and wipes settings). The cache fills up again with use, which is why it's the last step, not the first.

⚠️

Be wary of "booster" or "clean RAM" apps: closing processes doesn't free storage, and many exist just to show you ads.

FAQ

Is clearing the cache safe?

Yes: it only removes temporary files the app creates again. Don't clear an app's "data" or "storage" unless you want to reset it.

How much space can I recover?

It depends on your phone. In general, duplicates, heavy videos and messaging files give back the most, but there's no fixed figure: your gallery is different from anyone else's.

Can I move things to an SD card or the cloud?

If your phone has an SD slot you can move photos and videos. The cloud (Google Photos, Drive) frees local space, but it's best to clean duplicates first so you don't upload or pay for copies.
